# Codebook arm selection: the shipped heuristic was wrong at 16 KB

The swarm's 4th proposal, and the only one that asked for a measurement
rather than a design change: at d4-K2048 BOTH arms are legal
(16384 + 12288 = 28672 <= 32768, so the threadgroup arm is chosen today)
and their traffic profiles are opposite. Nobody had measured which is
faster. Its refuter was killed 30 s in on the grounds that refuting the
feasibility of running an A/B is close to vacuous.

Running it was worth more than every refutation of the day.

## The measurement

9k prefills, 5 reps, min reported, threadgroup -> CB_DEV:

| artifact | geometry | D | codebook | placement | threadgroup | CB_DEV | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 35B-3.4 | d4-K2048 | 4 | **16 KB** | single-box | 1316 t/s | **1942 t/s** | **1.475x** |
| 397B-2.6 | d4-K512 | 4 | 4 KB | 2-node | 335 t/s | 334 t/s | 0.997x |
| gemma-26b | d2-K2048 | 2 | 8 KB | single-box | 1209 t/s | 1194 t/s | 0.988x |
| 35B-5.4 | d2-K1024 | 2 | 4 KB | single-box | 1787 t/s | 1872 t/s | 1.047x |

**It is not D.** d4-K512 ties, so having four dimensions per code does not
by itself favour the device arm.

**It is not codebook size monotonically.** 8 KB ties while 4 KB is
marginally positive; a simple "bigger codebook -> device arm" curve is
falsified by its own middle point.

**The one decisive case is 16 KB — the LARGEST threadgroup-legal
codebook.** There the bulk load is maximal and leaves only 4096 B of the
32768 B budget spare. MECHANISM UNMEASURED: occupancy pressure at the
budget edge is the obvious candidate and is a hypothesis, not a finding.

Two of the four arms were measured 2-node, where rank->box assignment and
RDMA-vs-TCP are uncontrolled (see RTILE-2026-09-08.md). That only affects
the d4-K512 row, and a tie there is the claim least sensitive to it.

## Change

    cb_dev = _cb_bytes + 3*4096 > _TG_CAP_BYTES or _cb_bytes >= 16384

The budget still FORCES the device arm when the threadgroup copy cannot
fit; the new clause additionally prefers it at cb >= 16 KB where the
threadgroup arm is legal but slower. Everything below 16 KB is unchanged,
because nothing measured says it should change.

Verified END TO END with no env vars set — the default alone:

    35B-3.4, rebundled, 9k prefill:  1316 -> 1878 tok/s  (1.43x)

matching the forced-arm 1.475x within run-to-run noise.

## The clean demonstration: 397B-3.1

397B-3.1 is 171/171 pure d4-K2048 — the artifact with 100% exposure to
this change. It happened to be serving on the OLD runtime when the change
landed, so the before-arm was free and perfectly matched (same placement,
same session).

| 397B-3.1, 2-node, 9k prefill | min | tok/s |
|---|---|---|
| OLD (threadgroup, 16 KB cb) | 38.041 s | 182 |
| NEW (CB_DEV at >= 16 KB) | **21.596 s** | **320** |

**1.761x (+43.2%)**, non-overlapping. The largest single gain of the arc.

Caveats kept honest: 2-node, so rank->box and RDMA-vs-TCP are uncontrolled
(1.76x dwarfs that band, but it is not a clean-room number); and the new
arm's rep spread is 8.5% against the old arm's 2.4%.

## Fleet impact

~447 modules are d4-K2048: 397B-3.1 (171), Flash-3.2 (126), 35B-3.4 (120),
35B-4.6 (30). Those move to the device arm and take the gain. Artifacts
whose modules are all below 16 KB are untouched.

`VQ_MOE_GEMMSEG_CBDEV=0|1` forces either arm (0 is ignored where the budget
makes the threadgroup copy illegal); the flag exists so this A/B could be
run at all and is kept so it can be re-run.

Correctness: `test_gemmseg_cbdev_vs_threadgroup_equivalent` gates the two
arms at d4-K2048 within rel<1e-3. 24/24 pass.

## Why this was missed for so long

The original rule reads as an obvious economy — keep the codebook in fast
threadgroup memory whenever it fits, fall back to device memory only when
forced. It is right at small K and costs ~32% at the top of its range. It
was never measured because it never looked like a question.
